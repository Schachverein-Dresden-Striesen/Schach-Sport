# Morning Yoga Routine for Chess Players

## Overview

A consistent morning yoga routine prepares your body and mind for the day—and especially for chess. The classical Sun Salutation (Surya Namaskar) combines stretching, strengthening, and breathing, awakening your nervous system gently while improving posture and reducing the muscle tension that builds from hours over the board.

For chess players, a 10–15 minute morning yoga practice:
- Warms up your core and opens tight shoulders (from desk posture)
- Calms the nervous system without sedating you
- Improves focus and breathing before tournaments
- Prevents the slouch that degrades concentration

## The Science

**Posture and cognitive performance**: When you slouch, blood flow to the brain is slightly compromised, and your nervous system interprets the posture as "stressed." Upright, open postures trigger the parasympathetic nervous system (calm) and allow deeper breathing—both boost cognition.

**Breathing and focus**: Yoga emphasizes slow, controlled breathing (pranayama). This activates the vagus nerve, which regulates stress response. Studies show that deliberate breathing improves reaction time and decision-making under pressure.

**Flexibility and stress**: Tight muscles store tension. Gentle stretching releases physical tension, which often translates to mental relaxation.

## Practical Tips

### Surya Namaskar: Classical Sun Salutation (6 rounds)

This is the foundational flow. Repeat 6 times, synchronizing breath with movement.

1. **Mountain Pose (Tadasana)**: Stand tall, feet hip-width apart. Breathe in.
2. **Raise arms overhead**: Exhale, press palms together above your head.
3. **Forward fold**: Inhale, lower hands to the floor (or shins if hamstrings are tight).
4. **Half-lift**: Exhale, hands on shins, spine straight, gaze forward.
5. **Plank pose**: Inhale, step or jump back to plank. Shoulders over wrists.
6. **Knees-chest-chin**: Exhale, lower gently (not a push-up—elbows hug ribs).
7. **Upward dog (or cobra)**: Inhale, roll shoulders back, chest open.
8. **Downward dog**: Exhale, press hips up and back. Hold 5 breaths.
9. **Step forward**: Inhale, step feet between hands.
10. **Half-lift again**: Exhale, spine straight.
11. **Forward fold**: Inhale, fold forward completely.
12. **Return to mountain**: Exhale, roll up slowly, arms overhead, then to sides.

**Timing**: One round = ~1 minute. Six rounds = ~6 minutes. Do this 2–3 times per week, or daily if it feels good.

### Before Tournaments

On tournament days, do 3–4 rounds instead of 6. Stay calm and controlled; this is warm-up, not exertion.

### Common Mistakes

- **Rushing through the flow**: Yoga is synchrony of breath and movement. If you're panting, you're going too fast. Slow down.
- **Ignoring soreness**: If downward dog hurts your shoulders, modify: place forearms on the ground instead of hands.
- **Only doing it once**: Consistency matters more than intensity. 10 minutes daily beats 30 minutes once a month.
- **Skipping the breathing**: The breath is the whole point. If you're holding your breath, reset and start again.
